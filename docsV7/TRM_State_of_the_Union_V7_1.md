## Section 6 — The Structure Program

### dO Distribution

The O-step distribution `dO(t) = O(t+1) − O(t)` is the **mediator** between VC and all
emergent structure. It is generated by `ΔR = R(t+1) − R(t)`: when VC changes rapidly,
dO is large; when VC changes slowly, dO is small.

### Hierarchy from Heavy Tails

Hierarchy emerges from the **shape** of the dO distribution:

| dO Type | Depth | Mechanism |
|:--------|:-----:|:----------|
| Uniform | 1 | No surges → single scale |
| Gaussian | 4 | Few outliers → moderate depth |
| Heavy-tail (Pareto) | 5-6 | Frequent extremes → deep |
| Levy-like | 6 | Extreme-dominated → deepest |

**Mechanism:** Heavy tails produce rare large dO events ("compression surges"). Each surge
becomes a scale boundary in the coarse-graining hierarchy. More surges = more levels =
deeper hierarchy. Variance is necessary; tail heaviness determines depth.

### The Complexity Peak

Structure does NOT increase monotonically with R. It **peaks at R≈0.11** — the "edge of
ordering" where VC is strong enough to create compression but not so strong as to make it
uniform. At R=1, dO→0 and all structure collapses to flat uniformity. This is guaranteed
by **Rolle's theorem**: var(dO)=0 at both R=0 and R=1, so a maximum must exist between them.

| Method | dim growth | State cost | Mechanism |

1. The causal chain has out-degree=1 (deterministic)
2. Hierarchy adds at most 1 orthogonal axis (scale dimension)

topology). Both are available as extensions, but neither is needed for DSVC's core tasks
