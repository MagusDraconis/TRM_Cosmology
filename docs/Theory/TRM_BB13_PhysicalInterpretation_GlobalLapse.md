# BB13 — Physical Interpretation of the Global Lapse B(t)

---

## Context

CML01–CML17 established:

- B(t) acts as a uniform additive shift in phase dynamics
- B(t) is fully recoverable from observables
- B(t) propagates instantaneously (no delay)
- B(t) must be strictly global (any spatial variation breaks synchronization)
- B(t) is not generated by internal dynamics (no emergence, no feedback)
- B(t) separates cleanly from coupling and intrinsic frequencies

---

## Core Result

B(t) satisfies all of the following:

- global
- instantaneous
- dynamically inert
- non-emergent
- fully observable

---

## Logical Implication

B(t) cannot be:

- a local field
- a transported quantity
- a function of mass distribution
- an emergent collective property

---

## Candidate Interpretations

Evaluate the following:

---

### Candidate A — Gauge / Reference Time Shift

Interpretation:

    B(t) is a global normalization of time:

        dt_phys = λ(t) · dt_model

Mapping:

    B(t) = d/dt log λ(t)

---

### Candidate B — Background Clock Field

Interpretation:

    B(t) is a physically real global field
    defining the rate of time evolution

Constraints:

- no spatial variation allowed
- no propagation
- not dynamical

---

### Candidate C — Effective Medium State

Interpretation:

    B(t) is a global state of a medium

Test:

- must explain why it cannot vary spatially
- must explain absence of internal generation

---

## Evaluation Criteria

Each candidate must satisfy ALL:

- explains strict globality (CML15)
- explains zero propagation (CML17)
- explains non-emergence (CML16)
- explains full observability (CML13)

---

## Rejection Rules

Reject candidate if:

- requires finite propagation
- depends on local interactions
- predicts spatial variation
- requires feedback generation

---

## Key Question

What physical structure can exist that:

- affects all oscillators equally
- has no spatial degrees of freedom
- does not propagate
- is not generated by the system itself

---

## Expected Outcome

Either:

(A) B(t) is a gauge artifact (redefinition of time)

or

(B) B(t) is a fundamental global structure
    not described by local field theory

---

## Key Statement

B(t) is not a force, not a coupling, and not a field in the local sense.

It is either a global gauge degree of freedom
or a fundamental background time structure.

---

## Discriminating Test — Absolute vs Relative Observability

### Goal

Distinguish whether B(t) is:

(A) a gauge artifact (pure time redefinition)
or
(B) a physically real background field

---

### Principle

A gauge quantity is not observable in absolute terms.
Only differences or ratios are measurable.

A physical background must produce observable effects
independent of arbitrary normalization.

---

### Test Concept

Let two identical systems run with different imposed B(t):

    System 1: B1(t)
    System 2: B2(t)

Compare:

    Ω*_1(t) vs Ω*_2(t)

---

### Gauge Prediction

If B(t) is gauge:

- absolute B(t) is meaningless
- only differences matter
- both systems can be rescaled to match

Result:

    Ω*_1 and Ω*_2 are not physically distinguishable

---

### Physical Background Prediction

If B(t) is real:

- B(t) has absolute meaning
- two systems with different B(t) produce different observables

Result:

    Ω*_1 ≠ Ω*_2 (not removable by rescaling)

---

### Minimal Implementation Path

Simulate:

    System A: B(t) = 0.10
    System B: B(t) = 0.20

Check whether:

    ratio Ω*_A / Ω*_B is arbitrary
or fixed by dynamics

---

### Decision Rule

If difference is removable by scaling:

    → B(t) is gauge

If difference is absolute:

    → B(t) is physical background

---

## Key Decision Statement

The status of B(t) depends on whether it is:

- only a coordinate choice (gauge)

or

- an invariant measurable quantity (background field)
