# BB15 — Empirical Signatures of the Global Lapse B(t)

## Context

BB14 established:

- B(t) is global, instantaneous, non-emergent, additive, and observable
- not gauge (CML19, CML23)
- not local field (CML15–CML17)
- not system-dependent (CML20)
- acts as universal reference clock (CML21)
- history-independent (CML22)

The origin remains unresolved.

---

## Goal

Define observable signatures that distinguish:

(A) cosmological background origin  
(B) global constraint  
(C) fundamental background time  

---

## Principle

Each candidate must produce distinct observable predictions.

The goal is not explanation,
but falsifiable empirical differentiation.

---

## Observable Class 1 — Global Drift Over Real Time

Prediction:

If B(t) is cosmological:

    B(t) should evolve over time

Signature:

    Ω* slowly drifts even in isolated systems

Test:

    long-duration phase evolution measurement

---

## Observable Class 2 — Scale Independence

Prediction:

If B(t) is fundamental:

    effect is independent of system scale

Signature:

    identical ΔΩ across:
    - microscopic systems
    - macroscopic systems

Test:

    compare systems with different intrinsic frequency scales

## Candidate-Specific Predictions

### A — Cosmological Origin

- slow monotonic drift of B(t)
- linked to large-scale time evolution
- identical everywhere

Potential observable:

    cosmological redshift-like scaling in phase systems

---

### B — Global Constraint

- B(t) constant over time
- no natural evolution
- identical across all systems

Signature:

    no drift, only fixed offset

---

### C — Fundamental Background Time

- B(t) may evolve independently
- not tied to known physical quantities
- fully universal

Signature:

    drift exists but not correlated with other observables
