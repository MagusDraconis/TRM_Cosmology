## Interpretation A — Physical Background

Assumption:

    B(t) is a real, external field
    affecting the rate of time globally

Implications:

- all physical processes share a modified rate
- absolute time evolution differs from standard physics
- no local experiment can detect it directly
- indirect effects only through comparisons over time

Problem:

- violates principle of observability
- no operational procedure can measure B(t)

---

## Interpretation B — Time Redefinition

Rewrite time:

    t' = t + ∫ B(t) dt

Then:

    d/dt' replaces d/dt

Result:

- equations retain identical form
- all observables unchanged
- B(t) disappears from dynamics

Conclusion:

    B(t) is gauge-like at the global level,
    even if not removable locally in CML models

---

## Key Equivalence

A perfectly global B(t) is observationally equivalent to:

    a reparameterization of time

## Consequence

1. Universe with B(t)
2. Universe with redefined time coordinate
